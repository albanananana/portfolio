Case study · Bondata

A redesign that made survey results easy to act on, and 1.7× more managers now do.

Bondata is an employee-engagement analytics platform for HR leads and managers in Finland. It turns pulse surveys into results across ~40 views and 25 chart types, so teams can see where engagement drops and set objectives to fix it.

Goal

Raise the share of managers who set an objective within 14 days of a survey from 18% to 30%, within two survey rounds after launch. HR leads renew Bondata when their managers act on the results, so this was the number that mattered to the client.

Results at a glance

Problem

Companies buy Bondata so that line managers act on their team's survey results, but only 18% set an objective after a survey closed. The results were hard to reach: filters were hidden in a collapsed side panel and did nothing until you pressed Refresh, and cards hid the one number that mattered. The goal was to raise that share to 30% within two survey rounds.

Research

I planned and ran the research: the support tickets and usage analytics first, then interviews and a baseline test on the old product. Another designer took notes in every session.

Prototyping

I tested Figma prototypes with 8 people (3 HR leads, 5 managers) on the baseline's five tasks. Filters need real data, so I retested them on staging with a client's survey, again with 8 people. Task success and time in the results come from that round.

Design

I set the visual language and rebuilt the design system as a Material UI theme: 44 component sets, 136 colour variables, light and dark. Two designers applied it across part of the ~40 views under my review. Structure changed only where 82% of visits go: the dashboard and the results report. At twice-weekly client reviews I defended decisions with test results.

Decision 01

Filters were hidden and did nothing until Refresh

Before: find a collapsed side panel, open it, set up to 20 filters, press Refresh. Now filters sit in a row above the content and apply instantly. On scroll the row collapses into a sticky button; extra filters fold into "More".

Decision 02

An objective forgot the result that started it

The old card had a title, description and status, on a separate page. Now an objective carries its result: question, group, index, score and comments. "Set objective" on every results card turns a weak result into an objective without leaving the page.

Decision 03

The summary card had to be read, not scanned

The card was plain text at one size, benchmarks in brackets at line ends. Now response rate and comments are two figures on top, each strength and development area is a row led by its percentage with a green or red bar, and the benchmark is a tag. Downloads became links.

Decision 04

The AI summary didn't look like one

Three open paragraphs ended with "AI-generated summary may contain errors": you learned the source after reading. Now an "AI Summary" badge sits on top, the card wears the brand gradient no chart uses, and the text collapses to two lines behind "Read more".
